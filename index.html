How to use it

Three ways to speak. Pick whichever suits the moment.

With the keyboard

The easiest way. Nothing floating on screen.

  1. Tap any text box, then press the globe key until NextVoice is selected.
  2. Tap the microphone on the right of the top strip.
  3. Speak. The words appear in the field as you talk.
  4. Tap the microphone again, or just stop talking. The text is finished for you.

With the floating bubble

Optional. For when you prefer another keyboard.

  1. Switch it on from the home screen and allow the few extra permissions it explains, one at a time.
  2. Tap a text box in any app. The bubble appears.
  3. Tap it, speak, and tap again. The text is pasted where your cursor is.

Without internet

Set up once, then it just works.

  1. Open Settings → Voice Keyboard → Voice typing without internet.
  2. Tap Offline Bangla to download it once (about 94 MB, best over Wi-Fi).
  3. Leave the mode on Automatic. When there is no connection, the microphone listens on the phone by itself.
Type Bengali. Press the globe key to move between English, phonetic Bengali (type ami, get আমি) and the Bengali letters keyboard.
Rewrite anything. Select text in any app, open the toolbar (the four squares) and choose AI help to fix grammar, change the tone or translate.
Choose how it writes. Exactly as you said it, polished, or as bullet notes — set the style under Settings. Offline text is written exactly as heard.
Lost? Ask the app. A short tip appears the first time you open each screen, in your language, and Settings → About can show them again.

Installing

Four steps, once.

  1. Download the APK using the button above.
  2. Open it. Android will ask whether to allow installs from your browser — this is normal for an app that does not come from the Play Store. Allow it, then install.
  3. Choose Bengali or English on the first screen, then follow the short guided setup: microphone, notifications, turn the keyboard on and choose it. Steps are explained one at a time in your language, and any can wait. The floating bubble is optional and asks for its extra permissions only if you want it.
  4. Start dictating. It works straight away — no account or key needed. If you dictate a lot, add your own free key in Settings → Gemini Keys (from aistudio.google.com/apikey) or a Groq key from console.groq.com/keys; yours is used first.
If you already have an older build installed — one you got before this page existed — uninstall it first. Android refuses to replace an app when the new copy was signed with a different key. This is a one-time step; every version from here on updates normally.

Where your words go

Offline it stays on your phone. Online it goes to the AI service, not to us.

With no internet, or when you choose offline, your voice is recognised on the phone and nothing is sent anywhere. Online, your recording goes to the AI speech service that does the listening (Google Gemini, or Whisper on Groq), and the text may be sent to a text model to be polished or rewritten. NextVoice has no server of its own and does not keep your audio or your words. Signing in with Google is optional and only backs your settings up to a private document that only you can read.

The app carries several built-in Gemini keys and switches between them, so it works on install and every install shares the same free allowance. If you dictate a lot, or it says the limit was reached, add your own key in Settings. Groq and NVIDIA are available as optional alternatives.
